Most analytics tools answer “what happened?” in forty different charts. Orbit explores what happens when a console answers “what should we do next?” instead. This is a dummy write-up while the real case study is on its way.

## The problem

Product teams collect more signals than they can read. Dashboards grow, alerts get muted, and the decision that matters ends up buried one tab deep. The goal was to shrink the distance between a signal and a decision.

## The approach

Orbit groups signals around the questions a team actually asks: is activation healthy, where do people drop off, and what changed this week. Each view leads with a plain-language summary, then lets you drill into the data behind it.

- One summary per question, not one chart per metric.
- Every number links to the events that produced it.
- Changes are explained, not just plotted.

> “A dashboard is finished when someone stops needing to explain it.”

## What it could become

The concept stops at the interface, but the shape is clear: an event pipeline, a small query layer, and a summary engine that stays honest about uncertainty.

<Takeaway title="Where it could go">

Clarity beats coverage. A console that says less, and says it with context, helps a team move faster than one that shows everything.

</Takeaway>
